# Prime Fourier truncations

Haobo Ma (ChronoAI Pte Ltd, Singapore) and Wenlin Zhang (National University of Singapore; ChronoAI Pte Ltd, Singapore).

## Mathematical content

`note.pdf` and `note.tex` give a proof that deleting any two rows of a prime Fourier matrix of order at least five produces a non-isolated partial Hadamard matrix. More generally, for a nonempty proper row set of size m, the local dephased solution manifold has dimension (m−1)(p−m−1). The nonempty prime Fourier truncations are isolated exactly for m = 1, p−1, p.

The original question is Conjecture 1.14 on page 9 of Banica, Özteke and Pittau, arXiv:1706.00986v2. It is also Conjecture 8.36 in Banica, arXiv:1910.06911v9, pages 199–200. All references and the formal-statement links appear in the note.

Corollary 6 extends the defect calculation to every nonempty row set in every finite abelian group G of order N. If c_d(S) counts the cosets of the nontrivial cyclic subgroup generated by d that are entirely retained, the undephased defect is m(N−m+1) + sum_{d≠0} c_d(S). This answers Banica's Exercise 8.39. The orthogonality map is regular exactly when that sum is zero; then the analytic dephased dimension is (m−1)(N−m−1). A nonzero sum gives a tangent count, not an assertion of integrability or isolation.

Full prime Fourier rigidity is established prior mathematics. Tadej and Życzkowski attribute it to Petrescu's 1997 thesis and give a defect-based proof in their 2007 preprint, published in 2008. Banica's 2013 paper treats square finite abelian Fourier matrices; Banica, Özteke and Pittau's 2017 preprint supplies the closest partial-matrix tangent-space description. The note extends that framework and makes no priority claim.

## Finite checks

Python 3.8 or later and its standard library suffice:

```sh
python3 verify.py > verification.json
python3 verify_cyclic.py > cyclic-verification.json
```

The deterministic output enumerates the checked cases. The forest checks cover every nonempty proper subset for p = 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 and every nonzero step. Jacobian checks cover every nonempty subset for p ≤ 7 and the explicitly listed row sets for p = 11, 13. They include the one-row and full-matrix boundary cases.

For each p, the script chooses a prime q ≡ 1 (mod p) and an element of exact order p in its finite field. It reduces the cyclotomic coefficients of the ordered-pair Jacobian modulo q and computes rank by exact elimination. The two ordered rows for each unordered pair are equivalent, over the complex numbers, to the real and imaginary parts of that pair's differential. A nonzero minor after reduction certifies that the corresponding cyclotomic minor is nonzero; the written rank bounds give the matching upper bound. These are finite checks of the displayed cases, not a replacement for the all-prime proof.

The second script checks the cyclic defect identity on every nonempty subset for orders 2 through 12, and exact modular Jacobian ranks on every nonempty subset for orders 2 through 8 and the explicitly listed subsets for orders 9, 10, 12 and 15. Its 597 direct-product cases include every nonempty subset of Z₂×Z₂, Z₂×Z₃, Z₂×Z₄ and Z₂×Z₂×Z₂, together with nine explicitly listed subsets of Z₃×Z₃. Roots are checked to have the exact exponent of the group. Both scripts use integer arithmetic, without numerical precision or rounding choices.

## Coverage

The written proof gives the sharp dimension, one-row boundary, isolation classification, exact-velocity tangent integration and finite abelian defect formula. The pinned Lean declarations linked individually in the PDF cover cyclic incidence, prime proper-subset Jacobian surjectivity, rank–nullity, analytic level-set curves up to positive tangent scaling, two-row non-isolation and the negation of the uniform-density conjecture. The classification and finite abelian extension are written consequences rather than statements of the final Lean declaration. Singular composite-modulus isolation and the output matrices of the general arithmetic-isolation conjecture remain outside the conclusion.
